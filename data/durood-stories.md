## 1. Allama Iqbal's One Crore Durood

Dr. Muhammad Iqbal — the poet-philosopher who helped inspire the creation of Pakistan — had a deep, personal connection with durood. The story begins when he visited a friend who worked at the airport. He found his friend sitting with a tasbih, murmuring something. *"What are you reading?"* Iqbal asked. The friend said: *"Durood Sharif. Since I began reading it in large numbers, my life has changed."*

The friend then shared his experience. When he completed one lakh durood, he saw a dream: he was standing before a filthy drain filled with dirty water and garbage. He had to cross it. He waded through, struggling, until he reached the other side — where the ground became clean and pure. Then he saw the Prophet ﷺ sitting in a blessed gathering. The Prophet looked at him and smiled. The friend felt overwhelming peace. But then the Prophet said something that shook him: *"You will die in three days."* He woke up terrified. Three days later, he passed away — exactly as the Prophet had said.

Iqbal was deeply moved. That day, he made a firm intention: *"O Iqbal, from now on, durood will be your morning and evening wazifa. You will spend your life reading durood."* He began reading 10,000 Durood-e-Khizri daily. He kept reading and reading — until one day, he completed one crore durood. And not only that, he saw the Prophet ﷺ in a dream.

Later, when a doctor friend asked him: *"Iqbal, how did you become Hakim-ul-Ummat? How did you achieve this rank?"* Iqbal replied simply: *"The secret is that I read one crore durood in my life. You should read too — perhaps Allah will grant you a rank as well."*

## 2. Sultan Mahmood Ghaznavi: One Lakh Daily

Sultan Mahmood Ghaznavi — the legendary ruler — was not just a just king but a true lover of the Prophet ﷺ. He read one lakh durood every single day. The speaker describes how when you read with sincerity, Allah makes the wazifa easy for you and blesses your time. He mentions meeting a baba who reads 5,000 daily — by 2 PM his counter already showed 22,000. He starts after Tahajjud and reads until he sleeps.

But Sultan Mahmood's daily lakh eventually created a problem. He became so absorbed in durood that state affairs suffered. One night, the Prophet ﷺ appeared in his dream and said: *"You are reading too much durood. Reduce it."* The Sultan was shocked — how could the Prophet ask him to read less? But the Prophet ﷺ told him to reduce to 313 daily. Sultan Mahmood obeyed. He reduced his daily count to 313 — and from that day, his affairs returned to normal, and his kingdom flourished.

The speaker uses this story to explain that there is a balance. Not everyone is meant to read a lakh daily. What matters is consistency, sincerity, and intention. Even 1,000 daily, done with love, can transform your life. He says: *"When you worship with ikhlas for Allah's pleasure, Allah blesses your capacity. He makes the difficult easy."*

Since he began reading this way, his life transformed completely. His poverty vanished. The same relatives who had shunned him now greeted him warmly. His business flourished. His rizq increased enormously. He says: *"I was nobody. durood made me somebody."*

The speaker emphasizes that this is the power of durood read with presence of heart — not mechanically, but with the feeling that you are actually standing in the Prophet's ﷺ court. When you read with that love, the Prophet ﷺ remembers you, and Allah opens every door.
