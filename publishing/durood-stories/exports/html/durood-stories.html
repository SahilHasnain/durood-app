1. Allama Iqbal's One Crore Durood

Dr. Muhammad Iqbal — the poet-philosopher who helped inspire the creation of Pakistan — had a deep, personal connection with durood. The story begins when he visited a friend who worked at the airport. He found his friend sitting with a tasbih, murmuring something. "What are you reading?" Iqbal asked. The friend said: "Durood Sharif. Since I began reading it in large numbers, my life has changed."

The friend then shared his experience. When he completed one lakh durood, he saw a dream: he was standing before a filthy drain filled with dirty water and garbage. He had to cross it. He waded through, struggling, until he reached the other side — where the ground became clean and pure. Then he saw the Prophet ﷺ sitting in a blessed gathering. The Prophet looked at him and smiled. The friend felt overwhelming peace. But then the Prophet said something that shook him: "You will die in three days." He woke up terrified. Three days later, he passed away — exactly as the Prophet had said.

Iqbal was deeply moved. That day, he made a firm intention: "O Iqbal, from now on, durood will be your morning and evening wazifa. You will spend your life reading durood." He began reading 10,000 Durood-e-Khizri daily. He kept reading and reading — until one day, he completed one crore durood. And not only that, he saw the Prophet ﷺ in a dream.

Later, when a doctor friend asked him: "Iqbal, how did you become Hakim-ul-Ummat? How did you achieve this rank?" Iqbal replied simply: "The secret is that I read one crore durood in my life. You should read too — perhaps Allah will grant you a rank as well."

2. Sultan Mahmood Ghaznavi: One Lakh Daily

Sultan Mahmood Ghaznavi — the legendary ruler — was not just a just king but a true lover of the Prophet ﷺ. He read one lakh durood every single day. The speaker describes how when you read with sincerity, Allah makes the wazifa easy for you and blesses your time. He mentions meeting a baba who reads 5,000 daily — by 2 PM his counter already showed 22,000. He starts after Tahajjud and reads until he sleeps.

But Sultan Mahmood's daily lakh eventually created a problem. He became so absorbed in durood that state affairs suffered. One night, the Prophet ﷺ appeared in his dream and said: "You are reading too much durood. Reduce it." The Sultan was shocked — how could the Prophet ask him to read less? But the Prophet ﷺ told him to reduce to 313 daily. Sultan Mahmood obeyed. He reduced his daily count to 313 — and from that day, his affairs returned to normal, and his kingdom flourished.

The speaker uses this story to explain that there is a balance. Not everyone is meant to read a lakh daily. What matters is consistency, sincerity, and intention. Even 1,000 daily, done with love, can transform your life. He says: "When you worship with ikhlas for Allah's pleasure, Allah blesses your capacity. He makes the difficult easy."

Since he began reading this way, his life transformed completely. His poverty vanished. The same relatives who had shunned him now greeted him warmly. His business flourished. His rizq increased enormously. He says: "I was nobody. durood made me somebody."

The speaker emphasizes that this is the power of durood read with presence of heart — not mechanically, but with the feeling that you are actually standing in the Prophet's ﷺ court. When you read with that love, the Prophet ﷺ remembers you, and Allah opens every door.

Shocked, he asked: "Ya Rasool Allah! This man is a great sinner! Why are you holding his hand?"

The Prophet ﷺ replied: "Yes, he is a sinner. But every night before sleeping, he reads one thousand durood upon my blessed body. I am taking him to present him before Allah — and I will intercede for him."

When Allama Abdul Wahid woke and went to the mosque, the same sinful neighbour came to him, weeping. He said: "I saw the Prophet ﷺ in my dream. He was holding my hand and taking me to Allah for his intercession. And then he told me: 'Go to Abdul Wahid in the morning and take repentance at his hands.'"

The lesson: even a deeply sinful person who reads durood with regularity earns the Prophet's ﷺ special attention. Durood does not require perfection — it requires consistency. And through it, even the most fallen can find their way back.

7. The Woman Whose Coffin Wouldn't Lift

A very poor woman — she worked cleaning people's homes and sweeping school floors — had one great love: durood sharif. From her meagre monthly salary, she always set aside a portion for the service of durood. She was regular and disciplined in arranging durood gatherings in her home, inviting women to sit and read together.

When she passed away and her funeral was being lifted, something shocking happened — the coffin suddenly dropped and became impossible to lift. People were stunned. "This was a servant of durood! A righteous woman! What has happened?"

Among the women sitting in the gathering, an elderly, pious woman fell into a state — and saw the deceased woman standing before her, wrapped in her kafan. The elderly woman spoke to her: "I thought you were righteous! You read durood, you served it — what is this?"

The deceased replied: "I had a flaw. I did not pay my workers properly. I owe people money." The elderly woman told her to seek Allah's forgiveness and read durood. She did — and then the coffin became light. The funeral proceeded.

The lesson: durood is powerful, but it must be read with a clean heart, with ikhlas, and with justice in how we deal with others.

11. The Orphan Who Found Durood

A pious elder narrates his life story. He was five or six years old when his mother passed away. They lived in a rural area of India. After his mother's death, his father remarried. Their father, afraid of his new wife, offered no protection. The children lived in misery, praying each day that their stepmother would never return when she visited her parents' home — those were the only days their father showed them love.

One day, after the stepmother beat his younger sister severely, he complained to his father. In retaliation, she beat all the siblings brutally. Heartbroken, he ran away from home at the age of nine or ten. He boarded a train and traveled for hours before getting off at an unknown station. With only a few rupees, he spent his days wandering and his nights at the railway station. When his money ran out, he survived on leftovers that travelers threw away.

One day, extremely hungry, he picked up food from the ground. An elderly woman saw him, called him over, and fed him. She asked about his situation and, upon hearing his story, took him home. She bathed him, fed him, and gave him clean clothes. She asked him to read durood, but he didn't know what durood was. She taught him: "Read Sallallahu Ala Muhammad." He began reading it with her daily, and she taught him other prayers as well.

After some time, she told him it was time for him to leave and find his way in the world. She gave him blessings and sent him off. With the durood he had learned, he set out. In just a few days, he found work at a shop. The shopkeeper was amazed — he had never seen such a young boy work so well. He gave him food, shelter, and eventually sent him to school. The boy studied hard and grew up to become a respected, God-fearing man with a good job and family.

Years later, he went back to find the elderly woman who had saved him, but she was nowhere to be found. The neighbors told him no such woman lived there. He realized she was no ordinary person — she was a blessing sent by Allah through the medium of durood.
